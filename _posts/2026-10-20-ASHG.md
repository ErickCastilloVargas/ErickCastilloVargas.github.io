**Background**: The upper-airway microbiome is associated with inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) response and is influenced by host genetic variation. However, the role of epigenetic modifications, such as DNA methylation (DNAm), is unexplored. We aimed to identify DNAm markers in blood associated with the upper-airway microbiome implicated in asthma exacerbations despite ICS treatment.

**Methods**: We analyzed 242 European asthmatics from the GEMAS study in the discovery phase. We profiled the nasal, pharyngeal, and salivary microbiome by 16S rRNA (V3-V4) sequencing and blood epigenome using the Illumina EPICv1 array. We conducted microbiome epigenome-wide association studies (mbEWAS) to test associations between 731,223 CpGs and ICS-response-related microbiome traits (three diversity indices and 18 bacterial genera), while adjusting for age, sex, ancestry, cell heterogeneity, and batch. We identified differentially methylated regions (DMRs) using two complementary approaches. Replication was evaluated in nasal samples from 287 children from Project Viva. We conducted gene set enrichment analyses in biological pathways and traits. Significance was declared at FDR<0.05.

**Results**: Blood DNAm at 73 CpG sites was associated with upper-airway bacterial diversity and bacterial genera abundance in nasal (*Fusobacterium*, *Leptotrichia*, *Streptococcus*, *Rothia*) and pharyngeal samples (*Selenomonas*, *Tannerella*, *Campylobacter*, *Atopobium*) (FDR<0.05). We identified 227 and 347 DMRs associated with bacterial genera abundance and diversity, respectively (FDR<0.05). In nasal samples, we replicated two CpGs, including the *PTPRR* gene involved in corticosteroid sensitivity, and four DMRs, of which three mapped to *PTPRB* (*p*<0.05). Notably, the PTP gene family underlies asthma pathophysiology. Gene-set enrichment analyses highlighted asthma-relevant pathways (Wnt signaling, inflammatory response), related comorbidities (type 2 diabetes, obesity, and smoking), and genes likely regulated by trichostatin A, a candidate drug for asthma exacerbations suggested by genomic and microbiome studies.

**Conclusion**: The first mbEWAS in asthma identified novel associations between blood DNAm and the upper-airway microbiome linked to asthma exacerbations despite ICS use, including epigenetic loci involved in inflammation, asthma comorbidities, and potential therapeutic targets for asthma.

**Learning objective**: Identify methylation markers associated with the upper-airway microbiome linked to asthma exacerbations through a microbiome epigenome-wide approach.
