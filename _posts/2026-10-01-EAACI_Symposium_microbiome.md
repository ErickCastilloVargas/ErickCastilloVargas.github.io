**Background**: The upper-airway microbiome is associated with asthma exacerbations despite inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) treatment. Although human genetics is known to influence bacterial composition, no microbiome epigenome-wide association study (mbEWAS) has been conducted in asthma. Therefore, we aimed to identify whole-blood DNA methylation (DNAm) markers associated with the upper-airway microbiome related to asthma exacerbations despite ICS treatment.

**Methods**: We tested associations between whole-blood DNAm at 703,505 CpGs and salivary, nasal, and pharyngeal exacerbation-related microbiome traits in 242 asthma patients from GEMAS. We conducted differentially methylated region (DMR), gene-set enrichment, and quantitative trait loci (RNA-seq, n=119) analyses. Replication was evaluated in Project Viva (n=260) and SysPharmPediA (n=66) cohorts. We tested associations of the upper-airway microbiome with CpGs previously related to ICS response and/or asthma exacerbations and DNAm-predictors of plasma proteins. Significance was set at a false discovery rate (FDR)<0.05.

**Results**: Whole-blood DNAm at 73 CpGs and 550 DMRs was associated with exacerbation-related microbiome traits (FDR<0.05). DNAm markers at *PTPRR*, *GAL3ST2*, *TEX9*, *ACAT2*, *APCDD1L*, *ZBTB38*, *PTPRB*, and *AP5Z1* replicated in Project Viva or SysPharmPediA (*p*<0.05). We found enrichment in genes linked to asthma comorbidities (e.g., obesity), asthma-related pathways (e.g., TGF-β), and regulated by trichostatin A, NF-κB, and the glucocorticoid receptor (FDR<0.05). The CpG cg18994033 was associated with *ITM2C* blood expression. Seven CpGs previously associated with ICS response and/or asthma exacerbations and nine DNAm-predicted plasma proteins were associated with the exacerbation-related microbiome traits.

**Conclusions**: Blood DNAm markers implicated in inflammation, immunity, and asthma comorbidities are associated with the upper-airway microbiome linked to asthma exacerbations, supporting a role for microbiome-epigenome interactions in the exacerbations of this chronic respiratory disease.
